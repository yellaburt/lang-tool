Subjunctive highlighting

Live src/app.css, real DOM structure. Hues come from the same MOOD_HUES table and the same (pairId - 1) % 4 formula as views.tsx, so pair 1 is teal. Muted = mood trigger, saturated = subjunctive verb. Words are real <button class="word-clickable"> elements, so hover and tap feedback are testable here too.

1 · Four hue pairs, current chunk, no emphasis band

Each line is one pair. Can you spot the verb without reading for it?

  • Quiero que hoy hables con ella.
    I want you to talk to her today.
    Dudo que mi hermano coma carne.
    I doubt my brother eats meat.
    Te lo digo para que sepas la verdad.
    I'm telling you so that you know the truth.
    Es posible que ya haya llamado.
    It's possible he has already called.

2 · On the active emphasis band — the weak case

The active chunk paints its whole cell with --highlight-bg (yellow). The tint is alpha-composited over that, so it loses contrast. The first row below is active, the second is the same content unemphasized, for direct comparison — row 1 should read close to row 2. This is what killed amber: at hue 38 the trigger vanished into the band entirely, which is why MOOD_HUES now starts at teal. Check any new hue here before trusting it. Switch emphasis to bold or none to see the band disappear.

  • Quiero que hoy hables con ella, aunque sea tarde.
    I want you to talk to her today, even if it's late.
    Quiero que hoy hables con ella, aunque sea tarde.
    (same chunk, no emphasis band)

3 · Past sentence (dimmed text, flowing paragraph)

Past sentences dim via color, never parent opacity, so the tint keeps its full strength while the text recedes. Check the tint doesn't now shout louder than the words.

  • No creo que llames antes de que él llegue. Es mejor que esperes aquí.
    I don't think you'll call before he arrives. It's better that you wait here.

4 · Edge cases

A verb with no trigger (independent use), a trigger licensing two verbs (shared hue), and an unhighlighted cuando + indicative for contrast — the two-mood case where nothing should be tagged at all.

  • ¡Que te vaya bien!
    Have a good one! (verb, no trigger)
    Espero que comas y descanses.
    I hope you eat and rest. (one trigger, two verbs)
    Cuando llegó, ya no estaba.
    When he arrived, she was gone. (indicative — nothing tagged)

5 · Bare swatches, trigger vs verb

Same word at both weights in all four hues. Trigger should read as background, verb as foreground — if a trigger looks as loud as a verb, lower --mood-alpha-trigger.
